I am a DPhil candidate in Politics at the University of Oxford, supervised by [Federica Genovese](https://federica-genovese.com/) and [David Rueda](https://www.nuffield.ox.ac.uk/people/profiles/david-rueda/). My aspiration is to become a comparative political economist whose principal policy domain of interest is climate policy in rich democracies. In my thesis and related research, I examine how electoral incentives and organised interest groups interact when democratic governments address long-term policymaking problems, which involve imposing costs today for potential benefits in the future.

My other research concerns the political consequences of economic shocks, (right-wing) populism, and classic questions in comparative political economy, such as preferences for redistribution and the fiscal capacity of the state.

This website contains my [public writing and engagement](/writing/), [teaching materials](/teaching/), and details of my [professional service](/service/). In the [file drawer](/file-drawer/), I discuss my approach to research and share unsuccessful ideas and projects in the hope that others can learn from them.
