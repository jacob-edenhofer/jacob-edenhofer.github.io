I am a DPhil candidate in Politics at the University of Oxford, supervised by [Federica Genovese](https://federica-genovese.com/) and [David Rueda](https://www.nuffield.ox.ac.uk/people/profiles/david-rueda/). My aspiration is to become a comparative political economist whose principal policy domain of interest is climate policy in rich democracies. In my thesis and related research, I examine how electoral incentives and organised interest groups interact when democratic governments address long-term policymaking problems, which involve imposing costs today for potential benefits in the future.

My other projects fall into two categories. The first concerns (right-wing) populism. Across different papers, I examine the political consequences of economic shocks, including how rising energy prices and local economic decline affect support for the populist right, and how Brexit has affected regional economies. A further project analyses why electoral majorities delegate policy to technocrats when they fear losing power, and how this helps explain the populist backlash against technocracy. The second concerns classic questions in comparative political economy, including how inflation affects preferences for redistribution, whether parties target core or swing voters, how the progressivity of the background tax-and-transfer system moderates judgements about the fairness of (climate) policy, and how limited information and conflict between firms affect the state’s fiscal capacity.
